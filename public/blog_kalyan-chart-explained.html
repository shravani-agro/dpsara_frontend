Kalyan Chart Explained - How to Read Jodi and Pana Numbers

Understand Kalyan chart records, open close results, jodi history, and the methods users follow when analysing old chart data.

The Kalyan chart is the most widely followed historical record in the satta matka system, and for good reason. Every daily session produces an Open result and a Close result, and the Kalyan chart stores both in a structured format that stretches back years. But for most beginners, opening the chart for the first time produces genuine confusion: rows of 8-digit strings, columns labelled Mon through Sat, and terms like jodi, pana, Berij, and ank with no explanation attached.

This guide on Sara777 fixes that. By the end, you will know what every element of a Kalyan chart cell means, how to decode a real result, how to classify pana types on sight, and how to apply Berij and Farak analysis to the data you find. Every example in this article comes from the actual Sara 777 Kalyan chart archive.

What Is the Kalyan Chart?

The Kalyan chart is a historical record of every daily result from the Kalyan market, one of the oldest and most consistently followed markets in the satta matka system, established in Mumbai in the 1960s under the framework developed by Ratan Khatri. The market runs Monday through Saturday, with the Open result declared at 4:10 PM IST and the Close result declared at 6:10 PM IST. Every day between these two times, one session's worth of data is added to the chart on Sara777.

On Sara 777, the kalyan satta matka chart is a scrollable table. The first column shows the date range for each week (Monday to Saturday). The next six columns, one per weekday, each contain one result. That result on Sara 777 is not a simple kalyan matka number chart: it is an 8-digit string encoding three separate pieces of information. Understanding what those eight digits represent is the foundational skill every chart reader needs.

Understanding Jodi in the Kalyan Chart At Sara777

The jodi is the two-digit final result of each kalyan jodi chart session on Sara777, the kalyan matka number chart most players track daily. It is formed by placing the Open Single and Close Single side by side. Jodi 86 means the Open drew 8 and the Close drew 6. Jodi 05 means Open 0, Close 5. Jodi 00 means both drew 0, valid and confirmed in the archive, not an error.

There are 100 possible jodis (00 through 99) in kalyan satta matka chart on Sara777. Under random distribution, any specific jodi should appear approximately once every 14–17 weeks depending on how many days per week the market runs. When a jodi appears more frequently than this baseline, it is operating above random distribution, a frequency signal worth tracking. When it has been absent for significantly longer than average, it is a cold jodi, a gap signal worth monitoring.

Berij: The Most Useful Jodi Analysis Tool for Kalyan Jodi Chart

Berij is the digit sum of a kalyan jodi chart on Sara777. To calculate it: add both digits of the kalyan jodi chart and keep the last digit of the Kalyan total chart number.

Jodi 86 → 8+6=14 → Berij 4
Jodi 35 → 3+5=8 → Berij 8
Jodi 99 → 9+9=18 → Berij 8
Jodi 47 → 4+7=11 → Berij 1
Jodi 00 → 0+0=0 → Berij 0

Berij groups all 100 jodis into 10 families (Berij 0 through 9), with 10 jodis per family. When you track which Berij family has been dominant in the last 13 weeks, you are tracking a family-level frequency signal rather than individual jodi frequency on Sara777, a more stable and reliable analytical layer.

Kalyan Jodi Chart Farak: The Open-to-Close Gap on Sara777

Farak is the difference between the Open Single and Close Single of a kalyan jodi chart. For jodi 86: Farak = 8−6 = 2. For jodi 35: Farak = 5−3 = 2. For jodi 47: Farak = 7−4 = 3. Tracking the Farak across a week's results reveals whether the gap between Open and Close singles is widening, narrowing, or staying consistent, a directional signal that experienced chart readers use alongside Berij analysis.

Understanding Pana in the Kalyan Panel Chart on Sara777

Kalyan chart pana (also written panna or patti, three spellings of the same concept) is the three-digit kalyan matka number chart declared in each session phase. At Sara777, every kalyan panel chart session produces two panas: one at Open (4:10 PM) and one at Close (6:10 PM). The kalyan chart jodi is derived from the two panas. The kalyan panel chart, the 8-digit record, contains more analytical information than the jodi chart because it shows the source data behind each result.

Every kalyan chart pana belongs to one of three types on Sara777, identifiable by examining its three digits:

  • Single Pana (SP): All three digits are different. Examples from the archive: 260 (digits 2, 6, 0, all different), 178 (digits 1, 7, 8, all different), 359 (digits 3, 5, 9, all different). There are 120 possible SPs, the most common kalyan chart pana type, appearing in approximately 68.6% of all draws.
  • Double Pana (DP): Exactly two of the three digits are identical. Examples: 330 (two 3s), 448 (two 4s), 177 (two 7s). There are 45 possible DPs, appearing in approximately 25.7% of all draws. The repeated digit can lead (334), trail (133), or split (313), all three are DPs.
  • Triple Pana (TP): All three digits in kalyan panel chart identical. The complete list of all 10 TPs: 000, 111, 222, 333, 444, 555, 666, 777, 888, 999. There are no other TPs. They are the rarest kalyan chart pana type, approximately 5.7% of all draws, appearing on average once every 8–12 weeks per session side.

The TP ank correction: The Single of a TP is not always its repeated digit. The correct values: 000→0, 111→3, 222→6, 333→9, 444→2, 555→5, 666→8, 777→1, 888→4, 999→7. Always calculate rather than assume.

To classify any pana on kalyan panel chart: look at the three digits. All different = SP. Any two the same = DP. All three the same = TP. This visual check takes under five seconds and is the first analytical step when reading any kalyan chart pana result.

How to Read the Kalyan Chart: Step By Step

With the decoding method and pattern tools established, here is the complete reading workflow:

Step 1: Decode and verify the most recent cell: Take the most recent 8-digit entry. Split into Open Pana (positions 1–3), Jodi (positions 4–5), Close Pana (positions 6–8). Verify: kalyan matka chart open Pana digit sum last digit = jodi digit 1; Close Pana digit sum last digit = jodi digit 2. If both pass, the cell is valid. Time: 30 seconds.

Step 2: Classify the pana types: Apply the SP/DP/TP visual check to both kalyan chart open and Close panas. Note whether today's session was SP/SP, SP/DP, DP/DP, or included a TP. Update your rolling SP/DP/TP rate for the current 4-week window.

Step 3: Calculate Berij and Farak: From the kalyan chart jodi, calculate Berij (digit sum last digit) and Farak (absolute difference between kalyan chart open and Close singles). Note which Berij family today's result belongs to. Time: 15 seconds.

Step 4: Check the repeat gap for today's jodi: Note when today's jodi last appeared in the archive. Calculate the gap in weeks. If the gap matches the jodi's historical average interval, the repeat is confirmed as structural. If the gap is significantly above average, the jodi is in an extended absence, a cold signal. Time: 60 seconds.

Step 5: Update your column frequency tally: Add today's Open and Close singles to your running tally for this day-column (e.g., Monday's column tally, Friday's column tally). If any single has now appeared 4+ times in 20 sessions for this weekday, flag it as column-dominant. Time: 30 seconds.

Kalyan total chart time per session at Kalyan Chart: under 3 minutes. A player who completes this workflow every day builds a personal analytical record that compounds in value over weeks and months.

These eight digits follow a fixed positional format on Sara 777 used consistently across the entire archive:

So the result 26086330 tells you: the Open Pana drawn at 4:10 PM was 260, the final jodi was 86, and the Close Pana drawn at 6:10 PM was 330. Five pieces of information in one 8-digit string on Sara 777.

The connection between the Kalyan Chart panas and the jodi is mathematical, not coincidental. Add the digits of the Open Pana: 2+6+0 = 8. That 8 is the Open Single, and it is always the first digit of the jodi on Sara 777. Add the digits of the Close Pana: 3+3+0 = 6. That 6 is the Close Single, and it is always the second digit of the jodi. Jodi 86 = Open Single 8 + Close Single 6. The match is exact and verifiable for every cell in the archive.

This verification check on Sara777, Open Pana digit sum last digit must equal jodi digit 1, Close Pana digit sum last digit must equal jodi digit 2, is the quality control step that separates analytical use of the chart from passive browsing. Any cell that fails this check has a data entry error and should be excluded from pattern analysis.

Kalyan Chart Pattern Analysis: Four Methods with Real Examples

Kalyan Chart Pattern analysis on Sara 777 is the core skill of chart reading. Four methods apply directly to the Sara777 Kalyan archive.

Method 1: Jodi Repeat Gap
Every Kalyan Chart jodi has a historical repeat interval, the average kalyan matka number chart of weeks between its appearances in the archive. To calculate it for accurate kalyan chart pattern analysis: locate all appearances of the target jodi, note the row number of each, calculate the gap between consecutive appearances, and average them.

Method 2: Mirror Pair Tracking
A Kalyan Chart mirror jodi is a pair where the two digits are reversed: 12 and 21, 34 and 43, 86 and 68. From the Kalyan archive: jodi 86 appears in row 1 (Monday). Its mirror, jodi 68, can be tracked across the same archive for its most recent appearance. In most markets studied in this project, mirror jodis appear within 8–12 weeks of each other, a structural relationship rooted in the fact that they share the same Berij value (86→14→4; 68→14→4, both Berij 4). Players often compare these findings with the Kalyan Night Jodi Chart to identify whether similar mirror-pair movements are occurring across both sessions.

Method 3: Open Single Column Frequency
Because the kalyan satta matka chart is a Mon–Sat grid on Sara 777, each day-column can be analysed separately. Extracting every kalyan matka chart open Single from the last 20 active Monday rows gives a 20-value frequency distribution for Monday specifically. If kalyan chart open Single 3 appears 5 times in 20 Mondays (25%, against a 10% random baseline), Monday has a structural kalyan chart open Single 3 bias in the current phase. This column-level analysis is more precise than full-archive frequency because it isolates day-specific patterns.

Method 4: SP/DP Phase Detection
The theoretical ratio of SPs to DPs is 68.6%/25.7%. When a 4-week window on Sara 777 shows DPs above 30%, the Kalyan Chart market is in a DP-elevated phase, meaning Double Panas are appearing more frequently than their baseline predicts. During a DP-elevated phase, tracking the specific DP panas by their Single family narrows the candidate pool from 120 SPs to 45 DPs, a 62.5% reduction in tracking complexity. Detecting the phase shift is the analytical trigger.

Common Mistakes When Reading the Kalyan Chart

  • Misidentifying DPs as SPs: The pana 330 has digits 3, 3, 0, a DP. A quick glance may read “3 and 0” and classify it as SP. Always read all three digits individually before classifying. Back-doubled DPs (e.g., 133) and split-doubled DPs (e.g., 313) are the most commonly misclassified.
  • Assuming the TP Single equals its repeated digit: TP 777 has Single 1, not 7. TP 999 has Single 7, not 9. Only 000 (Single 0) and 555 (Single 5) have Singles that coincidentally match the repeated digit. Always calculate.
  • Treating a 4-week cluster as a repeat pattern: A kalyan chart jodi appearing 3 times in one week is a short-term cluster, not a repeat pattern. A structural repeat pattern requires at least three appearances in separate weeks with a consistent gap interval. Acting on a cluster as if it is a structural pattern is the most common analytical error in chart reading.
  • Skipping blank cells when counting intervals: The Sara777 Kalyan archive contains confirmed blank weeks, full Diwali suspension weeks (visible as ******** across all 6 columns) and single-day suspensions. A blank week is a real elapsed week. When counting the gap between two jodi appearances, count every row including blank rows. Skipping blank weeks produces gap intervals that are shorter than the actual elapsed time.
  • Checking only the jodi chart and ignoring the pana chart: The jodi is the output. The pana is the source. A player who tracks only jodis misses the Open Pana SP/DP/TP classification, the pana family membership, and the Single verification check, three analytical layers that the pana chart provides and the jodi chart does not.

Time: 15 seconds.

Frequently asked questions

What does an 8-digit Kalyan chart cell actually contain?+−

It encodes three elements in fixed positions: Open Pana (digits 1–3), Jodi (digits 4–5), and Close Pana (digits 6–8). Example: 26086330 means Open Pana 260, Jodi 86, Close Pana 330. The kalyan chart jodi digits are always the last digit of each pana's digit sum.

What is Berij and how do I calculate it for a kalyan chart jodi?+−

Berij is the digit sum of a jodi, keeping only the last digit. Jodi 86: 8+6=14, Berij 4. Jodi 35: 3+5=8, Berij 8. It groups jodis into 10 families of 10, useful for family-level frequency tracking.

What is the difference between Kalyan Day and Kalyan Night charts?+−

Kalyan Day runs Monday to Saturday with Open at 4:10 PM and Close at 6:10 PM. Kalyan Night is a separate session with different timings and an independent archive. The two charts cannot be used interchangeably for pattern analysis, they are distinct markets.

How many pana combinations exist in the Kalyan system?+−

There are 175 Kalyan total chart panas: 120 Single Panas (all digits different), 45 Double Panas (two digits identical), and 10 Triple Panas (all digits identical). The complete TP list is: 000, 111, 222, 333, 444, 555, 666, 777, 888, 999.

How is the Kalyan Final Ank Chart calculated?+−

The kalyan final ank chart comes from the Close Pana — it is the last digit of the Close Pana's digit sum. For Close Pana 330: 3+3+0=6, Final Ank = 6. For Close Pana 457: 4+5+7=16, Final Ank = 6. For Close Pana 179: 1+7+9=17, Final Ank = 7. It always equals the second digit of the session's Jodi. This also works as a quick accuracy check — if your calculated kalyan final ank chart does not match the second digit of the Jodi shown in the chart, that cell has a data entry error.

How do I pick a strong number pair for Kalyan?+−

A structurally strong jodi satisfies three conditions: it has appeared 3 or more times in the current 13-week window (above the random baseline of approximately 0.65 appearances); its most recent appearance falls within ±2 weeks of its calculated average repeat interval; and it belongs to a Berij family currently showing above-average frequency in the archive. A jodi meeting all three conditions has frequency, cycle, and family-level evidence pointing toward it simultaneously, the strongest signal the chart data can produce.

How do I find the kalyan matka chart open Ank, Jodi, and Panel number in Kalyan?+−

Every Kalyan session result on Sara777 is stored as an 8-digit string. The first three digits are the kalyan matka chart open Pana (Panel), digits 4–5 are the Jodi, and digits 6–8 are the Close Pana. The Open Ank is found by adding the three digits of the Open Pana and keeping only the last digit, for example, Open Pana 260: 2+6+0=8, so the Open Ank is 8. This single digit is always the first digit of the Jodi.

How to calculate Kalyan Jodi tricks?+−

Add both digits of any jodi to get its Berij, jodi 86 gives 8+6=14, Berij 4. Jodis sharing the same Berij belong to one family and tend to cluster together in the archive. Tracking which Berij family is currently active is the most reliable jodi analysis method.
